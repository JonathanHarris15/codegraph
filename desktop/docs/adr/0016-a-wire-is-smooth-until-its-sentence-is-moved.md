# 16. A wire is smooth until its sentence is moved

Date: 2026-11-06

## Status

Accepted. Changes what ADR 0010 says about a moved sentence.

## Context

A wire is drawn through its sentence, so the two cannot come apart. Two things
bent wires nobody had asked to bend. A sentence that was moved stayed where it
was put even when a card its wire joins was dragged far away, so it held the
wire like an anchor and pulled it out of shape. And a sentence nobody had moved,
stepping aside to keep clear of something, bent its wire too, so a wire changed
shape as things were dragged past it.

## Decision

A wire is one smooth curve from card to card. A sentence sits on that curve:
to keep clear of something it slides along the curve, and it never steps off it.

Only moving the sentence itself bends the wire.

Moving a card lets go of any moved sentence on a wire that card joins, from the
moment the card is picked up. The sentence goes back on the line and moves along
with it, and the arrangement or sketch forgets where it was put. This holds
however the card is moved, by hand, arrow key or alignment, and when both of the
wire's cards move together.

## Consequences

A sentence placed by hand is lost when one of its cards moves. We chose that over
keeping its distance from the line, because a wire bent out of shape would then
stay bent wherever the cards went, and moving a card is now how a wire is made
smooth again.

On a level too crowded for any spot on the line to be clear, a sentence stays in
the middle of its wire, on top of whatever is there, until a person moves it.
It no longer steps to one side to find room.
